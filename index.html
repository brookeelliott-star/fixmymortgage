The trap: 5 in 6 clicks are informational, and 61% come from NMLS-lookup queries (license verification, zero course intent). Buyer tiers are ~13% of keywords but under 3% of clicks because they all rank page 4–6. Demand exists; ranking does not.
1

Wrong audience

The blog ranks best for the people least likely to buy. NMLS license-verification queries sit at ~position 8 and pull most of the clicks; every buyer-journey topic is buried on page 4–6. Roughly 2 in 3 clicks have zero purchase intent.

2

Fragmented & buried buyer content

Not a content gap, a cannibalization problem. 19 “become an MLO [state]”, 19 exam-prep, 7 background-check, 7 requirements, 6 “how long” posts split authority across thin near-duplicates. “Become an MLO” averages pos ~43; “Requirements” ~51 with zero clicks on ~6,000 impressions.

3

No conversion path + technical drag

On-funnel readers arrive and leave: encyclopedia entries, no commercial framing, no route to a course CTA. On top, index bloat (7,564 “crawled – not indexed”, 605 noindex, 212 hard 404s, 201 soft 404s) suppresses the money pages.

Ground rules to bake into every edit
  • No em dashes anywhere, and no hyphens in prose.
  • No year references and no pricing in evergreen body copy.
  • External links only to .gov sources. No competitor links.
  • Internal link anchor text three words or fewer, woven naturally into a sentence, never a naked button in body copy.
  • No fabricated stats or testimonials.
  • Everything must be Craft CMS and Redactor compatible. Use HTML comment placeholders for modules, avoid tables where Craft does not handle them.
  • Every CTA points somewhere real and carries a UTM link.
